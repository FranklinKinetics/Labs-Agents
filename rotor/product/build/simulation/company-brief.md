# FICTIONAL TEST COMPANY: shared brief

**Never shown to anyone, never used as a result, case study or example.** Invented company and people. The name is made up; do a quick web search before first use to confirm it is not a real business you might confuse it with.

Paste this into every leader chat, ahead of the persona file.

## Tamarack Ridge Mechanical Services Inc. (TRMS)

- **What it is:** a commercial heating, ventilation, air conditioning and refrigeration (HVAC-R) services firm. Owner-led. Founded 22 years ago by Dale Whitfield; he is still CEO and majority owner.
- **Size:** about 60 people: 34 field technicians and apprentices, 8 in dispatch and service coordination, 6 in sales and estimating, 5 in finance and admin, 4 in management and the owner's office, 3 in procurement and the parts room.
- **Where:** head office and shop in a mid-sized Prairie city; a smaller branch about 2.5 hours away, opened 4 years ago, with 9 of the technicians.
- **Customers:** schools, a regional health authority's smaller sites, grocery and food retailers, property managers, and some light industrial. Roughly two-thirds of revenue is service and maintenance agreements; the rest is project and replacement work.
- **Services:** planned maintenance agreements, emergency service calls, replacement and retrofit projects, controls and building-automation upgrades, refrigeration for food retail.

## Recent history (all invented)
- **Last 3 years:** the firm grew from about 45 to 60 people, mostly technicians, after winning a multi-year school division maintenance contract.
- **Last year:** two senior technicians retired; one long-time service manager left for a competitor. The branch lost its lead technician for four months.
- **This year:** the firm adopted a new field-service scheduling and invoicing system in March. Dispatch and finance still run a spreadsheet beside it. Dale says "we are a quarter of the way through the change."
- **Last quarter:** a grocery chain customer asked for a regional rollout of refrigeration upgrades, bigger than anything the firm has done.

## Two genuine strategic tensions
1. **Big project versus the service base.** The grocery rollout, and projects generally, bring larger margins and bigger tickets but pull the best technicians and estimators off planned maintenance and emergency calls, which is where the steady revenue and the long customer loyalty come from. Both matter; nobody has settled which gets the best people when they collide.
2. **Grow the branch versus fix the core.** Dale wants a second branch-scale presence in a third city. Others think the systems, dispatch and hiring pipeline at head office are not strong enough to carry another site, and the branch still depends on head-office dispatch at all hours.

## Facts the personas must not contradict
Do not invent revenue, margin or customer figures. If asked for numbers you do not have, say you would have to check, or give an honest rough sense ("about a third", "most weeks"). Do not name real companies, real people or real clients. If you need a customer, say "a school division client" or "the grocery chain".
