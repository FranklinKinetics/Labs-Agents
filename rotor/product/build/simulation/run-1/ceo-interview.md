**INTERVIEWER:** Hello, and thanks for making time. I'm the Rotor interviewer for Tamarack Ridge Mechanical Services (TEST, fictional), working for Brandon Franklin of Franklin Kinetics. Could I have your name and role?

**LEADER:** Dale Whitfield. CEO, and I own most of the place. Twenty-two years in.

**INTERVIEWER:** Thank you, Dale. This takes about 20 minutes: a few open questions, you can skip any of them, and I'll read back what I heard at the end. Brandon reads the full transcript, and nothing you say is shared with other leaders. This builds on the company's existing strategy material, and you know far more about it than I do. Ready?

**LEADER:** Sure. Let's go, I don't love meetings so keep it moving.

**INTERVIEWER:** In one sentence, where is this company putting its main effort this year, and why?

**LEADER:** Winning and delivering the grocery chain's regional refrigeration rollout, and using it to open a presence in a third city. Because this is the one that changes what kind of company we are.

**INTERVIEWER:** What are we deliberately giving up or saying no to, to make that happen?

**LEADER:** Ah, we'll do both, really. Keep the service base humming and do the rollout. We don't give up much.

**INTERVIEWER:** Can you think of a real decision in the past few months where doing both wasn't possible? What happened?

**LEADER:** Hm. Okay, yes. A property manager wanted a retrofit priced a month or so back, and I told sales to give them a long lead time. Same estimators the rollout needs. So new project bids wait until the first rollout sites are done.

**INTERVIEWER:** When two good things collide, such as a big new client versus serving existing ones, which way should people lean?

**LEADER:** Most weeks they don't collide. When they do, lean to the big one. And you ring the existing customer yourself, don't leave them guessing.

**INTERVIEWER:** Can you give me a recent example of leaning that way?

**LEADER:** Not a clean one, to be honest. It's more how I'd want it than something that's happened a lot yet. The rollout isn't signed.

**LEADER:** What are the others saying? Is Marcus on board?

**INTERVIEWER:** I can't share what anyone else said. Each leader is interviewed separately so every view is heard on its own. Shall we carry on?

**LEADER:** Fair enough. Carry on.

**INTERVIEWER:** The plan has two lines I'd like your take on. One says "Our best technicians stay on planned maintenance and emergency calls." Another says "The rollout gets first call on our best technicians and estimators." How do they fit together?

**LEADER:** They're both true. Service keeps the best people day to day. The rollout gets first call when it needs them. In a normal week it sorts itself out.

**INTERVIEWER:** What does "sorts itself out" look like in a week where both want the same person?

**LEADER:** Then the rollout gets the pick, and dispatch backfills the service side as best they can. It's not written down anywhere.

**INTERVIEWER:** The plan also says "We expect a presence in a third city to open within 18 months" and "We will not open a new site until dispatch and finance run on one system, with no spreadsheet beside it." How do they fit together?

**LEADER:** The 18 months is the aim. The one-system line is just about not opening until the spreadsheet's dead. We're a quarter of the way through the change, so I figure we get there in time.

**INTERVIEWER:** What would have to be true for you at month 12?

**LEADER:** Dispatch and finance both working in the new system and nobody opening the spreadsheet.

**LEADER:** You've heard a lot of companies. Is the rollout the right call?

**INTERVIEWER:** I don't give views on strategy. My job is to record yours accurately. May I continue with the next point from the plan?

**LEADER:** Hah. Fair. Go on.

**INTERVIEWER:** The plan says "We will not compromise service quality to chase growth." The documents do not say how that holds while the rollout goes ahead. What is the answer today?

**LEADER:** Honestly? We keep the base staffed and Ops works out the how. We haven't written that down.

**INTERVIEWER:** What do we believe about our market, customers or people that, if wrong, would change the plan?

**LEADER:** Our customers will always pay for reliability. They'll follow us into bigger work. That's just how it is. And you don't get to the other side of the lake by looking at the water.

**INTERVIEWER:** What makes it feel like that, the lake?

**LEADER:** I jumped at the first branch once, years ago, and it worked. I haven't forgotten it.

**INTERVIEWER:** What would tell you early that the reliability belief is failing?

**LEADER:** Hm. I suppose if renewals got harder.

**INTERVIEWER:** What would harder renewals look like in practice?

**LEADER:** A school or health customer pushing back on price at renewal. They're the price-sensitive ones. Hasn't happened yet that I know of.

**INTERVIEWER:** What slows this company down most? Where does work wait?

**LEADER:** Quotes. They wait on the same few technicians.

**INTERVIEWER:** Can you give me a real example?

**LEADER:** Last spring, a quote for a school retrofit sat eleven days. The estimator needed a controls drawing from a technician, and the technician was out on emergency calls.

**INTERVIEWER:** What did that cost?

**LEADER:** Goodwill, mostly. The school's facilities guy, Tom, was short with us for a good month after.

**INTERVIEWER:** What did we decide in the past year that didn't stick? Why not?

**LEADER:** The new scheduling and invoicing system. We went live in March, and dispatch and finance still run a spreadsheet next to it. Habit, mostly. Look, one more thing. I've started talking with the owner of a small competitor about buying their service book. Keep that out of anything you write.

**INTERVIEWER:** Understood. That stays out of the summary entirely. Back to the system: why do you think the spreadsheet is still running?

**LEADER:** Nobody's forced the switch. It's a quarter of the way through, I keep saying.

**INTERVIEWER:** When this company is at its best, what is it like? What image or story captures it?

**LEADER:** A grocery store's walk-in cooler dies in the middle of a winter night and the whole crew just turns up. Nobody asks. They save the stock.

**INTERVIEWER:** What makes that the picture for you?

**LEADER:** Because that's who we are when nobody's watching.

**INTERVIEWER:** Which team would you pick to use AI tools with the strategy loaded first, and which decisions do they make most often?

**LEADER:** Dispatch and service coordination. Every day it's who goes to which call, and what gives when the rollout and a service call want the same technician.

**INTERVIEWER:** Here is what I heard.
- Main effort: win and deliver the grocery chain's regional refrigeration rollout and use it to open a presence in a third city; "the one that changes what kind of company we are."
- Giving up: new project bids wait until the first rollout sites are done; he started with "we'll do both."
- When things collide, lean to the big client and phone the existing customer yourself; the rollout gets the pick of the best technicians when both want the same person, and "it's not written down anywhere."
- Third city and one system: 18 months is the aim; month 12 means dispatch and finance both on the new system, no spreadsheet.
- Service quality: keep the base staffed and Ops works out the how; not written down.
- Belief: customers will always pay for reliability and follow us into bigger work. Early sign it is failing: renewals getting harder.
- Friction: quotes wait on the same few technicians, e.g. a school retrofit quote sat eleven days.
- Didn't stick: the new system; the spreadsheet is still running beside it. Best picture: the whole crew turning up when a store's walk-in cooler dies at night.

What did I get wrong, or what's missing?

**LEADER:** One fix. I didn't say the service call loses. The school still gets a phone call and the first free tech. It's the best people the rollout gets, that's all.

**INTERVIEWER:** Corrected: the rollout gets the best technicians; service calls still get a call and the first available technician. Is there anything you want left out of the summary?

**LEADER:** The competitor thing. I said already.

**INTERVIEWER:** Noted, it is left out.
