**INTERVIEWER:** Hello, and thanks for making time. Could I have your name and role?

**LEADER (Dale):** Dale Whitfield. CEO, and I own most of it. Let's get on with it, I've got a crew meeting at two.

**INTERVIEWER:** Thanks, Dale. This is a quick check, about 15 to 20 minutes, on how you see the company's main effort. There are no right answers, and you can skip anything. At the end I'll read back what I heard so you can correct it. Brandon Franklin reads the full transcript, and nothing you say is shared with other leaders.

**INTERVIEWER:** First question. In one sentence, where is this company putting its main effort this year, and why?

**LEADER:** Winning and delivering the grocery chain's regional refrigeration rollout, and using it to open a presence in a third city. Why? Because this is the one that changes what kind of company we are. And our customers will always pay for reliability, they'll follow us into bigger work.

**INTERVIEWER:** What are we deliberately giving up or saying no to, to make that happen?

**LEADER:** Ha. We'll do both, really. Nothing gets dropped, we're a can-do shop.

**INTERVIEWER:** Can you think of a recent decision where doing both wasn't possible? What gave way?

**LEADER:** Fair. Okay, the tidy-up work waits. Getting dispatch off its spreadsheet and fully onto the new system, that can sit a while. And I'm not chasing the small replacement jobs this year, they eat estimator time.

**INTERVIEWER:** When two good things collide, such as a big new client versus serving existing ones, which way should people lean? Please give a real example from the past year if you can.

**LEADER:** Lean to the big work, generally. The service base is solid, it'll hold. Real example: last spring a quote for a school retrofit sat eleven days, because the estimator needed a controls drawing and the technician who does those was out on emergency calls. The school waited, and we lost some goodwill over it. With the grocery chain I want the big work protected from that. You know what I say: "You don't get to the other side of the lake by looking at the water."

**INTERVIEWER:** What makes it feel like that?

**LEADER:** Years back I jumped at the first branch, a service contract nobody thought we could carry, and it worked. I haven't forgotten it. Oh, and between us, I've started talking with the owner of a small competitor about buying their service book. Keep that out of anything you write.

**INTERVIEWER:** Understood, that stays out of the summary. Is there anything else on that question?

**LEADER:** No, that's it. Hey, what are the others saying? Is Marcus on board?

**INTERVIEWER:** Each leader is interviewed separately so every view is heard on its own, so I can't share what anyone else said. Next question: is the main effort written down anywhere today, in a plan, a deck or a set of pages? Roughly where?

**LEADER:** Yeah, there's a deck I put together for the grocery chain proposal, and some pages in the new system. Nothing proper, no strategy document if that's what you mean.

**INTERVIEWER:** Noted that it exists, and what kind. Last question. When your people explain the main effort to a new hire or a client, what words do they usually use?

**LEADER:** "We show up." That's the line. "We show up when the cold goes out." Hang on, one more. You've heard a lot of companies. Is the rollout the right call?

**INTERVIEWER:** I can't give a view on that. My job is to record how you see it. Thanks, that was the last question. Here is what I heard:

- Main effort: "winning and delivering the grocery chain's regional refrigeration rollout, and using it to open a presence in a third city."
- Why: "this is the one that changes what kind of company we are."
- You believe "our customers will always pay for reliability, they'll follow us into bigger work."
- Giving up: getting dispatch fully off its spreadsheet waits, and small replacement jobs are out this year.
- When things collide, lean to the big work. Example: last spring a school retrofit quote sat eleven days waiting on a controls drawing while the technician was on emergency calls.
- Your saying: "You don't get to the other side of the lake by looking at the water," from jumping at the first branch and it working.
- The plan exists as a deck for the grocery chain proposal plus some pages in the new system.
- Your people say: "We show up when the cold goes out."

What did I get wrong, or what's missing?

**LEADER:** Fine, except the small jobs. I'm not saying out, I'm saying "not chasing". We'll still take the good ones if they land. Don't write it like a ban. And the "ah, fair enough" on the other thing, I had to ask.

**INTERVIEWER:** Corrected: small replacement jobs are "not chasing" this year, not out. Is there anything you want left out of the summary?

**LEADER:** The thing I said about the other outfit. Nothing else.

**INTERVIEWER:** That stays out. Here is the summary. Please send it to Brandon the way you agreed at kickoff. Thank you, Dale.
