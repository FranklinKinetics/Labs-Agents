# Scoped proposal: template

Fenn, 2026-11-12. The "scoped proposal" Brandon sends after a fit check ("Start a conversation", step 2: *scope before price*). Two variants share one structure. Pick one, delete the other, and fill every bracket. Sources: [rp2b-spec.md](../../rp2b-spec.md), [offer.md](../../package/offer.md), `rotor/beta-playbook.md` (Beta structure, deliverables, measures, governance), `rotor/website-copy.md` ("Your data", "After the Beta"), and `logs/decisions.md` to 2026-11-11.

**Before sending, check:**
- No fee, range, discount, credit or "founding rate" anywhere. The fee line stays a placeholder until Brandon agrees the number with the client, in writing, before work starts.
- No ROI or financial promise. No other client named, quoted or hinted at.
- "What we heard" uses the client's own words from the discovery call and the fit check. Leader quotes are unattributed unless that leader agrees.
- The two offers are never compared, in either variant, on price or on how much of Brandon.
- The vendor retention period is filled in from the vendor's current policy (Distillation).

---

## Shared structure

1. What we heard
2. What we'll do, and in what order
3. What you'll have at the end
4. What we'll ask of your team
5. Your data, in plain words
6. What it doesn't promise
7. After the work
8. Fee and terms
9. Next step
10. *(Optional)* You'd be among the first

## Variant A: the Rotor Distillation

> # The Rotor Distillation: proposal for [COMPANY]
> **Franklin Kinetics · prepared for [CEO NAME] · [DATE]**
>
> ## 1. What we heard
> [Two to four sentences in their words. What prompted the conversation; where their strategy lives today (EOS, OKRs, a plan, offsite notes); what the fit check showed about their leaders' main effort; how their people use AI today.]
>
> [One sentence naming the gap, e.g.: "Your leaders agree on [THEIR MAIN EFFORT, IN THEIR WORDS]. The AI tools your people use every day have never seen it."]
>
> ## 2. What we'll do, and in what order
> About 12 weeks, starting [START DATE]. Three calls, all with you, in the evening or late afternoon. Everything else runs on your time.
>
> | When | What happens |
> |---|---|
> | Before week 1 | You run your strategy material ([LIST: plan, EOS or OKR pages, offsite notes, values]) through a reading guide in your own AI, and email me the digest it produces. |
> | Week 1 | **Kickoff call** (45 min, you and me). Scope, what's off limits, which team, which AI tools, what we'll measure. |
> | Weeks 1–2 | You have one 20-minute conversation with an AI interviewer, in your own AI. [One or two more leaders may too: NAMES or "optional".] |
> | Weeks 2–3 | I draft your Rotor Sheet from your own strategy: your main effort said more sharply, the trade-offs you've already named, the beliefs your plan rests on with a warning sign for each, and your company's own language. I flag gaps and contradictions; I don't fill them in. |
> | Week 3 | Your leaders read the draft and send a short written reaction. |
> | About week 4 | **Sign-off call** (60 min, you and me). Open questions settled; you sign off the main effort and the Rotor Sheet. |
> | About week 5 | [IT CONTACT] loads the sheet into [AI TOOLS] using a step-by-step guide. [TEAM LEAD] writes ten real dilemmas, and we test the AI against them before anyone relies on it. |
> | Weeks 5–11 | **Six weeks of live use** by [TEAM]. An update from me every two weeks, one short set of questions to the team, and a reply to every message within three working days. |
> | About week 12 | Results pack, then the **review call** (60 min, you and me). Keep it, extend it to more teams, set up a quarterly review of the sheet, or stop. |
>
> ## 3. What you'll have at the end
> - **Your Rotor Sheet**, distilled from your own strategy, in your own words, signed off by you.
> - **[TEAM]'s AI tools set up** to work from the Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core, tested against ten real dilemmas from that team.
> - **A plain-language summary of how the AI behaves**, and a short note for your staff, so everyone can see what it works from.
> - **Updates every two weeks** during the live weeks.
> - **A results pack**: how good the AI's guidance was in your team's own judgment, whether people can name the main effort and two guardrails, the moments where the guidance changed a call, and where people overrode it.
>
> **The main effort stays yours.** I help you say it more sharply. I don't choose it, and neither does the AI.
>
> ## 4. What we'll ask of your team
> - **You:** the three calls, one 20-minute AI interview, and running your strategy material through the reading guide.
> - **Your leaders ([NAMES]):** a short written reaction to the draft sheet. [One or two may also do a 20-minute interview.]
> - **A beta team of at least five people** ([TEAM], named at kickoff) who work with the sheet for six weeks and answer one short set of questions. At least five, so no result ever points at one person. [TEAM LEAD] writes the ten test dilemmas.
> - **[IT CONTACT]:** time to load the sheet into your AI tools, around week 5.
>
> ## 5. Your data, in plain words
> - Your leaders' interviews, and the reading of your strategy documents, run in your company's own AI platform ([PLATFORM]), under your existing agreement with that vendor.
> - I receive two things by email: the short summary at the end of each interview, and a structured digest of your strategy documents. Your AI produces both. I never receive your documents or the full conversations.
> - I do my drafting in my own AI account (a one-person plan), in one private project used only for your company. Training on that account's content is switched off in its settings, and I confirm that in writing before the first interview.
> - At close-out, I delete that project, the emailed summaries, the digest and every copy I hold, and send you written confirmation. The AI vendor may keep deleted content for [X days, from the vendor's current policy].
> - The Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core go into your AI tools under licence.
> - Quotes from your leaders are never attributed unless that leader agrees. Results are reported by team, never about a person, and are never used in performance reviews.
> - Anything you name at kickoff ([EXCLUSIONS ALREADY NAMED, or "acquisitions, people decisions, legal matters, unannounced numbers"]) stays out of every interview and every document.
>
> ## 6. What it doesn't promise
> No financial results. Six weeks can't show up on the books, and I won't pretend otherwise. I report what happened, including where nothing changed.
>
> ## 7. After the work
> - **You keep your Rotor Sheet.** It's yours.
> - **The Rotor Core is licensed for the engagement.** The licence ends when the work ends, unless you renew it.
> - At the review we decide together what comes next: keep it, extend it to more teams, set up a quarterly review of the sheet, or stop. If the live weeks show your leaders splitting on the main effort, we'll talk about the Rotor Beta. Nothing renews on its own.
>
> ## 8. Fee and terms
> - **Fee:** [FEE: fixed, agreed before work starts]
> - **Payment terms:** [TERMS]
>
> ## 9. Next step
> If this looks right, reply to this note and we'll agree the fee and the start date in writing. I'll then send the welcome pack and the note on how your data is handled, and book the kickoff for [PROPOSED DATE AND TIME]. If anything here doesn't fit, tell me and I'll change it or tell you plainly why I can't.

## Variant B: the Rotor Beta

> # The Rotor Beta: proposal for [COMPANY]
> **Franklin Kinetics · prepared for [CEO NAME] · [DATE]**
>
> ## 1. What we heard
> [Two to four sentences in their words. What prompted the conversation; what the fit check or the diagnostic showed about where their leaders' main efforts differ; what that's costing them day to day, in their words, never in dollars; how their people use AI today.]
>
> [One sentence naming the gap, e.g.: "Your strategy is written down, but your leaders would describe [TWO DIFFERENT DIRECTIONS], and your people feel it."]
>
> ## 2. What we'll do, and in what order
> About four and a half months, starting [START DATE]: a build, one live quarter with one team, and a review. Three live meetings, booked at kickoff; everything else runs on your schedule.
>
> | When | What happens |
> |---|---|
> | Week 1 | **Kickoff** (60–90 min, you and [BETA SPONSOR]). Scope, measures and the rules for your data agreed. The AI interviewer set up on your platform. The Mirror and the review booked. |
> | Weeks 1–5 | **Leader interviews, in three short rounds**, each leader on their own time, by voice or text, with an AI interviewer in your own AI. An AI reads your existing strategy documents. I read every transcript, find where your leaders agree and where they don't, and draft candidate main efforts and guardrails for them to react to. |
> | About week 5 | **The Mirror** (2–3 hours, your leadership team). Your strategy reflected back in your leaders' own words; the contested calls settled; the Rotor Sheet signed off. |
> | Weeks 5–6 | [TEAM LEAD] sends real dilemmas; I run them against the AI and tune it; the lead rates the answers. The team goes live only once this passes. |
> | Weeks 6–18 | **A full quarter of live use** by [TEAM]. You brief the team at go-live (a recorded video works). A short weekly update from me, monthly pulses, and a reply to every message within two working days. [COMPARISON TEAM] works as usual, so we can tell the Beta's effect from people simply trying harder. |
> | About week 19 | **Beta review** (90 min, your leadership team). Results against the measures; scale, adjust or stop. |
>
> ## 3. What you'll have at the end
> - **A summary of where your leaders agree and where they don't**, and the language your company already uses.
> - **Where people, money and attention actually go**, set against the main effort.
> - **Your signed-off Rotor Sheet**, including the language map.
> - **A one-page Rotor visual** and **a two-to-three-minute strategy video** that you narrate, with short follow-up clips per guardrail.
> - **[TEAM]'s AI tools set up** with the Rotor Core and your Rotor Sheet, tuned in the dilemma test, with a plain-language summary of how the AI behaves published internally.
> - **A short note for your staff** on what the AI carries.
> - **Weekly updates** through the build and the live quarter.
> - **A review report** with recommended changes.
>
> **The main effort stays yours.** Your leaders settle it at the Mirror. I help you get there and say it sharply; I don't choose it, and neither does the AI.
>
> ## 4. What we'll ask of your team
> - **You and [BETA SPONSOR]:** the kickoff, the go-live briefing (or the video), and the review.
> - **Your leaders ([NAMES]):** three short rounds of AI interviews on their own time, the Mirror, short reactions to the draft, and the review.
> - **A beta team of at least five people** ([TEAM]), named at kickoff, who work with the sheet for a quarter and answer short pulses and a set of decision cases at the start and the end. [TEAM LEAD] writes the test dilemmas.
> - **A comparison team** ([COMPARISON TEAM]) who answer the same pulses and decision cases, and otherwise carry on as normal.
> - **[IT CONTACT]:** time to set up the AI interviewer and load the sheet into your AI tools.
>
> ## 5. Your data, in plain words
> - Your strategy stays in AI tools your company already uses ([PLATFORM]), under your existing agreements with those vendors.
> - Franklin Kinetics does not host your strategy data.
> - Interview transcripts are read only by me and stay in your own AI platform. Before the first interview, every leader is told what will be attributed and what stays anonymous. Quotes are never attributed unless that leader agrees.
> - At the end of the engagement, I delete the interview material from my notes.
> - Everyone in the company can see the strategy the AI works from and a plain-language summary of how it behaves.
> - Results are reported by team, not by individual.
> - Anything you name at kickoff ([EXCLUSIONS ALREADY NAMED, or "acquisitions, people decisions, legal matters, unannounced numbers"]) stays out of every interview and every document.
>
> ## 6. What it doesn't promise
> No financial results. What a quarter can show is whether your people decide more consistently with your intent than the comparison team, whether they can name the main effort and its guardrails, and how good the AI's guidance is in their own judgment. Any financial effect is a question for later reviews to test, not something I'll promise. I report what happened, including where nothing changed.
>
> ## 7. After the work
> - **You keep your Rotor Sheet.** It's yours. [CONFIRM: the visual and the strategy video stay with you too.]
> - **The Rotor Core is licensed for the engagement.** The licence ends when the work ends, unless you renew it.
> - At the review you decide what comes next: scale it, adjust it, or stop. If you continue, the options are quarterly reviews of the sheet and a company-wide rollout, each scoped separately. Nothing renews on its own.
>
> ## 8. Fee and terms
> - **Fee:** [FEE: fixed, agreed before work starts]
> - **Payment terms:** [TERMS]
>
> ## 9. Next step
> If this looks right, reply to this note and we'll agree the fee and the start date in writing. I'll then book the kickoff for [PROPOSED DATE AND TIME], and the Mirror and the review with it. If anything here doesn't fit, tell me and I'll change it or tell you plainly why I can't.

## Optional block: you'd be among the first

> **You'd be among the first.**
>
> I want to be straight with you before you decide. The Rotor Distillation is new. The method behind it draws on the Rotor Beta and the Rotor Framework, but this format hasn't run with a client before. Yours would be among the first companies to take it.
>
> **What that means for you**
> - Everything on the offer sheet still applies: the fit check, the three calls, the reply standard, the data rules and the written deletion at the end.
> - Some of the materials will change as we go. When something doesn't work, I'll fix it and tell you what I changed.
> - I track my own time on every step. That's how I find out what the work really takes, and it's my problem, not yours.
>
> **What I'd ask in return**
> - **Honest feedback, as we go and at the end.** At the close, three short questions to your CEO and the beta team lead: was the sheet true to your strategy, did the AI's guidance help, and what was annoying. Blunt answers help most.
> - **The rest is your choice.** Any testimonial, quote or write-up about your company would come only later, if you want it, and only with your separate written permission. Saying no changes nothing about your Distillation.
