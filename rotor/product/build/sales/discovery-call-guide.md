# Discovery call guide: "Start a conversation"

Fenn, 2026-11-12. For Brandon, not for clients. Sources: [rp2b-spec.md](../../rp2b-spec.md) (fit check, ladder, risks), [offer.md](../../package/offer.md) (ladder wording, founding note), the Distillation fit check in [review-checklists.md](../checklists/review-checklists.md), the "Discovery questions" and "Objections" in `rotor/beta-playbook.md`, and `logs/decisions.md` to 2026-11-11.

**The job of this call:** find out which question this company is really asking. *"We agree, but our AI tools have never seen it"* points to the Rotor Distillation. *"My leaders don't agree, and it shows"* points to the Rotor Beta. *"I don't know whether they agree"* points to the alignment diagnostic first. Sometimes the answer is none of them, and saying so is part of the service.

**Rules for the call**
- Listen more than you talk. Roughly 20 of the 30 minutes are theirs.
- Never compare the offers on price or on how much of you each one includes. Sort them by one question only: do the leaders already agree?
- No fees, ranges or ROI. If they ask "what does it cost?", say: "It's a fixed fee, agreed in writing before any work starts. You'll see the scope first, then the fee." If they ask "what will it earn us?", say: "I can't promise financial results, and I won't. I can show you how well the AI's guidance holds up in your team's own judgment, and I'll report it plainly, including where nothing changed."
- No client names, results or stories. There aren't any yet.

---

## The 30 minutes at a glance

```
 0–3   Open                  who they are, why now
 3–20  Questions             six to eight, below; follow the energy
20–25  Your read, out loud   which one fits, or none, and why
25–30  What happens next     fit check, then a proposal
```

---

## Opening line

> "Thanks for making the time. I've got no slides and no pitch. I'd like to spend most of this half hour understanding what's happening in your company, and at the end I'll tell you plainly whether I think I can help, and how. That includes telling you if I don't think it fits. So: what made you reach out now?"

Then stop talking. The first answer usually carries the real reason.

---

## The questions

Ask them in roughly this order, but follow the thread they give you. You won't always need all eight.

| # | Ask | Why you're asking |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"What made you reach out now?"** *(the opening)* | The trigger. An AI rollout, a missed plan, a leadership fight and a board nudge are four different calls. |
| 2 | **"Is your strategy written down? Where: EOS, OKRs, a plan, offsite notes?"** | The Distillation starts from what they've already written. No written strategy means there's nothing yet to distill. The fit interview doesn't cover this, so this call has to. |
| 3 | **"If I asked five of your leaders separately for the main effort, would I get the same answer?"** | The sorting question. Listen to *how* they answer, not just what. |
| 4 | **"When two good priorities collide, who decides, and how do people know which way to lean?"** | Shows whether trade-offs are settled and shared, or live in one head. |
| 5 | **"What did you decide at your last planning session that didn't happen?"** | Separates "the plan is clear but doesn't reach daily work" from "the plan was never really agreed". |
| 6 | **"Which AI tools are your people using today, officially or not? And who looks after IT?"** | The Distillation needs people already using AI at work and someone who can load the sheet. "Not officially" is common and fine. |
| 7 | **"When this company is at its best, what is it like?"** | Listen for metaphors and repeated phrases. They become the language map, and they tell you how the CEO thinks. |
| 8 | **"What would have to be true at the end for this to have been worth it?"** | Checks expectations. If the answer is a revenue number, reset it gently now (see the ROI line above). |

If there's time and it fits, add one: *"What belief is your current strategy built on that would hurt most if it turned out wrong?"* It's a good test of whether they can think about their plan from the outside.

## What to listen for

| You hear | It points to |
|---|---|
| A specific answer to Q3, said with confidence: "Yes, they'd all say [X]." A real plan on EOS, OKRs or paper (Q2). The pain is downstream: "people don't use it", "the AI gives generic answers", "we tried pasting the plan into ChatGPT". | **Rotor Distillation** |
| "Can't we just upload our strategy deck to ChatGPT?" | **Rotor Distillation.** This is its doorway. "That gives the AI a document, and vague documents produce vague guidance. The Distillation gives it your main effort, your trade-offs and the beliefs to watch, in your own words." |
| Q3 answered with a laugh, a pause, or "depends who you ask". Q4: "I decide, and they guess." Q5: the same priority missed twice. Names a leader who "isn't on board". Two leaders would describe different companies. | **Rotor Beta.** The split is the real problem; the AI can wait. |
| "I *think* they'd agree, but I've never asked." Several leaders and no shared plan document. The CEO wants evidence before committing the leadership team to anything. | **The alignment diagnostic first.** Each leader answers on their own; they see where they agree and where they don't, and it's useful even if they stop there. |
| A written plan exists, but Q3 and Q4 don't match it. | Probably the **Beta**, or the **diagnostic first** to find out. Don't push the Distillation onto a plan nobody shares; that's the main risk in the spec. |
| "We want you to tell us what our strategy should be." | Neither offer does that. The main effort is always theirs. If they want the hard calls made *with* them, that's the Beta. If they want someone to choose for them, it's neither. |

**Remember:** this call doesn't decide the Distillation alone. The fit check does (see "What happens next"). This call decides whether a fit check is worth anyone's time, or whether to point straight at the Beta or the diagnostic.

## Saying "I don't think this fits"

Say it plainly, say why, and point somewhere useful. People remember the consultant who said no.

**When it's the Beta, not the Distillation:**
> "From what you've told me, I don't think your leaders agree yet on where you're going, and that's normal. If we put the strategy into your AI now, it would carry a plan your own team doesn't share. The Rotor Beta settles that with your leadership team first, then carries the result to your people and your AI tools."

**When it's the diagnostic first:**
> "Honestly, I don't think either of us knows yet whether your leaders agree, and I'd rather find out than guess. The alignment diagnostic does exactly that. Each leader answers on their own, you run it yourselves, and you see where you agree and where you don't. If it shows you're aligned, we talk about the Distillation. If it shows a split, we talk about the Beta. And if it's all you need, that's fine too."

**When it's neither:**
> "I don't think I'm the right help for this, and I'd rather tell you now than sell you something that doesn't fit. [The reason, in one sentence.] What I'd suggest instead is [a book, a person, a tool, or simply waiting until ___]."

Common reasons it's neither:
- The company is well outside roughly 20 to 200 people.
- Their people don't use AI at work and don't plan to, and the leadership question is settled.
- They want an AI, or Brandon, to choose the strategy for them.
- They want a financial guarantee.
- They want to track individual employees.
- The thing that matters most right now is something we'd have to keep out of every interview anyway (an acquisition, a dismissal, a legal matter).
- There's no one with the time: the CEO can't give three calls, or no one can put together a team of five.

## What happens next (say this in the last five minutes)

> "Here's what happens from here. If you've taken the alignment diagnostic, its report is our fit check. If not, I'll send you a short note on how your data is handled, and then you and one or two of your leaders each have one 20-minute conversation with an AI interviewer, in your own AI. I read the short summaries, not the conversations. That tells us whether your leaders really do name the same main effort. If they do, you get a written proposal: what we'll do, in what order, what you'll have at the end, and what's asked of your team. Scope first, then the fee. If they don't, I'll tell you, and we'll talk about the Beta or the diagnostic instead. That call is mine to make, and I make it myself."

If it's clearly the Beta from this call, say the proposal comes next, and agree when you'll send it.

**For the first companies:** say the founding-client wording from [offer.md §4](../../package/offer.md) in your own words, and repeat it in writing with the proposal.

**Times:** offer evenings first, then 4 pm.
