## Where your data goes

- **Interviews and the document helper run in [COMPANY]'s own [PLATFORM] account**, under your existing agreement with that vendor. The full interview conversations stay there. Brandon does not receive or read them.
- **What Brandon receives, by email:** each leader's interview summary block, the CEO's extraction block from the document helper, and, if the CEO chooses to send it, the company's alignment diagnostic report. The summary blocks follow the same rule for the fit check before signing. **Brandon never receives the strategy documents themselves, or any full transcript.**
- **Who reads what.** Only Brandon reads the blocks. What reaches the leadership team is a summary of where views agree and differ. Quotes are unattributed unless the leader agrees otherwise.
- **Where drafting happens.** In Brandon's own AI account: one private project for [COMPANY], named by a code, used for no other client. Training on that account's content is switched off in its settings. Brandon confirms the setting in writing before the first block arrives. [CONFIRMATION DATE]
- **What stays out.** No one in [COMPANY] needs to create an account for Brandon.
- **Close-out.** At the end of the engagement, Brandon deletes the project and every copy he holds, including the block emails in his inbox, and sends [COMPANY] a written confirmation by [DATE, within X working days of close-out]. The vendor's own retention for deleted content is [X days, confirmed before kickoff].
- **Brandon's own notes.** Brandon keeps method notes about how his own tools perform. They contain no names, no quotes and no strategy content.
- **The Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core** go into [COMPANY]'s own AI tool for the beta team's project only. The Core is licensed and is not to be shared outside that project.

## How the AI behaves

The AI in the beta team's project keeps quiet unless something matters. It asks questions rather than giving instructions. It assumes people know more about their situation than it does, and raises issues in proportion to the stakes. When a choice clearly crosses an agreed guardrail, it says so plainly and hands the decision back to the person. It accepts a decision once the person has made it, and never steers covertly. A person can always override it.

## What this program does not do

- It does not report on any individual. Pulse answers are read only by Brandon and reported by team.
- It is not used in performance reviews.
- It makes no promise about financial results. It measures how well people know the strategy and how the guidance rates, and reports the results honestly, including where nothing changed.
- It does not choose the company's strategy. It starts from the one you already have.
