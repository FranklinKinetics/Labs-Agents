# Results video script (T13)

**Purpose:** show the whole leadership team the same evidence, honestly, before the CEO review, so the review is spent deciding rather than presenting.
**Target length:** 4 to 5 minutes (roughly 600 words spoken). Shorter is better. Cut any measure that wasn't agreed at kickoff.
**Adapt time:** about 15 minutes, after you've checked the results pack (A12). Every number comes from the pack. If it isn't in the pack, it isn't in the video.
**Sent:** at least [three] working days before the CEO review on [date].

**Fill before recording**
- [company] · [CEO first name] · [review date]
- The measures agreed at kickoff, each with the beta team result and the comparison team result
- For the headline measure (the noise audit), pick the one block below that matches the result, and delete the other two.

**Hard rules for this video**
- Team-level results only. No individual is named or identifiable.
- No financial or ROI claims. Financial effects stay hypotheses for the quarterly reviews.
- A null or mixed result is reported as plainly as a good one, in the same tone, at the same length.
- Say what one team over one quarter can't show.
- The decision is [CEO first name]'s, with you, at the review. Give your read as yours, and mark it.

---

[Direction: seated, plain. Charts on screen as simple bars: beta team and comparison team, before and after. Same scale for both teams. No animation that flatters.]

Hi everyone. It's Brandon. This is what happened over the last quarter, so you all see the same evidence before [CEO first name] and I meet on [review date] to decide what's next: scale it, adjust it, or stop.

**How to read it** [about 30 seconds]

A reminder of the setup. The [beta team] worked with the Rotor Sheet and the AI for twelve weeks. The [comparison team] carried on as usual. Both answered the same decision cases at the start and at the end. Comparing the two teams matters, because people often improve just from being watched. These are early measures of how decisions are made. They can move in a quarter. They are not financial results, and I won't pretend they are.

**The headline: how consistently the team decides** [about 1 minute]

[Direction: one chart. Spread of answers, before and after, both teams.]

[IF A CLEAR DIFFERENCE] At the start, the [beta team]'s answers to the same cases were [spread at start]. At the end, [spread at end]. The [comparison team] went from [x] to [y]. So the beta team's answers came closer together, and the comparison team's [did / didn't] by as much. That's one team over one quarter. It's a real signal, not proof.

[IF MIXED] Some of it moved and some didn't. On [type of case], the beta team's answers came closer together: [x] to [y]. On [type of case], they didn't: [x] to [y]. The comparison team went from [x] to [y]. My best guess at why is [reason]. That's a guess, and the review is where we test it.

[IF NO DIFFERENCE] The honest answer is that we didn't see a difference here. The beta team's answers were about as far apart at the end as at the start: [x] and [y]. The comparison team: [x] and [y]. That's a real result, and it's worth as much as a good one. The likeliest reasons I can see are [reason one] and [reason two]. I could be wrong about both.

**The supporting measures** [about 1 minute]

[Direction: one line each, one small chart each. Only the measures agreed at kickoff.]

- **Leader agreement on the main effort:** [number] of [number] leaders named the same main effort before the Mirror; [number] of [number] after.
- **Recall:** [share] of the beta team could name the main effort and two guardrails at week [12], against [share] of the comparison team.
- **Where time goes:** [share at start] to [share at end] of the beta team's sampled hours on the main effort. Comparison team: [x] to [y].
- **Decision speed:** [recurring decision]: [time at start] to [time at end]. Comparison team: [x] to [y].
- [For any measure that didn't move: "This one didn't move: (x) to (y)." Say it in the same voice as the others.]

**What the AI got right, and what it got wrong** [about 45 seconds]

[Direction: real examples from the decision log and pulses only, anonymised to the team. One of each if you have them. If you don't have one, say so.]

Where the guidance helped: [example, by situation, not by person]. Where it got in the way: [example]. Where it missed: [example]. [The team rated (share) of its answers as right, rather than too pushy, too quiet or wrong.]

**What the sheet told us** [about 30 seconds]

Tripwires: [none fired / (number) fired: (which belief) and what it signalled]. Repeated overrides: [none worth noting / people kept overriding (guardrail), which suggests (it's too tight / it's unclear / local knowledge it doesn't allow for)]. Friction found: [the one or two sources of delay named, each with its owner].

**What I'm not claiming** [about 15 seconds]

This doesn't tell us what any of it is worth in dollars. [The financial beliefs we wrote down at the start] stay beliefs until the quarterly reviews test them. And one team is one team.

**My read** [about 30 seconds]

[Direction: say plainly that this is your view.]

My read, for what it's worth: [scale to (which teams) / adjust (what, specifically) / stop]. Because [one or two reasons, tied to the results above]. If you see it differently, you know [company] better than I do, and that's exactly what the review is for.

[If you'd change the sheet, the visual or the video: "Whatever we decide, I'd change (line) because (reason)."]

**Close**

Please watch this before [review date], and send [CEO first name] or me any question you'd want answered there. Thank you for a quarter of honest work. [Optional, only if true: one specific thing you saw the team do well.]

[Direction: cut. No music.]
