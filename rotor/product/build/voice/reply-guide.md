# Reply guide

Every memo or question gets a reply within **three working days**. Responsiveness, not availability: the client can't book your time, but they can count on an answer.

## Order of work
1. Anything the client flagged as urgent goes first. If you can't answer it fully that evening, send one line saying when you will.
2. Then oldest first.
3. If day three will slip, send a one-line holding reply before it does, with a date. Never let the standard lapse silently.

## Write or record?
**Write** when the answer is a fact, a date, logistics, or something they'll want to find again (who sees what, what was agreed).
**Record a short video** (under 3 minutes, phone, one take) when:
- the question is about meaning, not facts;
- the person sounds worried or annoyed;
- you want to try out images and metaphors with them (save the commitment to one for the Mirror);
- a written answer would run past about 200 words.

## Tone
- Answer the question in the first line. Context after.
- They know their business better than you do. Ask before you assume.
- Plain, short sentences. Their words, not Rotor jargon.
- Thank them once, briefly, when it's earned.
- AI may draft. You read it and send it in your own words. Nothing goes out unread.

## Never, in a reply
- Say what another leader said, or confirm or deny that anyone else raised the same thing.
- Settle the main effort or a contested guardrail. That's the leadership team's call, with you, in the room.
- Promise a result, or put a number on what it's worth.
- Mention another client.

## "That's for the Mirror"
Use it when a question is really one of the open calls. Don't use it to dodge.
1. Say the question is real.
2. Say why it belongs in the room: settling it with one person settles it for everyone.
3. Give them something to do with it now.

> That's a real question, and it's one the whole team needs to settle, so I'd rather not answer it for you here. It's on the list for the Mirror. Could you bring one example from the last few months where it mattered? That will help the room more than my view will.

## A question you can't answer yet
Say so, say what you need, and give a date. Never guess on privacy, data, platform terms or anything contractual.

> I don't know yet, and I'd rather check than guess. I'm confirming [what] with [whom / the vendor's terms] and will come back to you by [date].

## Outside the program's scope
Answer briefly if you can. If it's bigger than a reply, say so and offer to raise it at the CEO review.
