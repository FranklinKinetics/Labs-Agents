# Pre-Mirror video script (T7)

**Purpose:** tell the leadership team what the Mirror is for, what is already agreed, what the room must settle, and how to come ready, so the two hours go to the hard calls.
**Target length:** about 3 minutes (roughly 420 words spoken).
**Adapt time:** about 15 minutes. Do your Mirror prep first; the brackets come straight from it and from the pre-read.
**Sent with:** the pre-read (divergence summary and what the Mirror must settle), at least [three] working days before the Mirror.

**Fill before recording**
- [company] · [Mirror date, start time] · [remote link / your office, room]
- [number] lines agreed out of [number] drafted, from wave 3
- [two to four contested questions], each written as a choice, never as a position someone holds
- [number] main-effort candidates in the pre-read

**Check before recording**
- [ ] No contested question can be traced to one leader by its wording, role or example.
- [ ] You haven't hinted at which candidate you prefer.

---

[Direction: seated, calm. This is a briefing, not a pitch. Speak to the whole team.]

Hi everyone. It's Brandon. The Mirror is on [Mirror date], at [start time], [on the video link in your invitation / at (place)]. Two hours. Here's what it's for and how to come ready.

[Direction: on screen, one line: "One page your people can act on without asking."]

What it's for. Over the last [number] weeks each of you has told me, separately, how you see [company]'s strategy. On [Mirror date] you'll see it reflected back in your own words, settle what's still open, and sign off one Rotor Sheet: one main effort, the guardrails, and the beliefs you're betting on, each with a tripwire. The test for every line is simple. Could a new hire, or an AI tool, act on it without asking you?

What's already agreed. More than you might think. Of the [number] lines in the draft, [number] drew no real objection in your last round of replies. They're marked "agreed" in the pre-read. In the room we'll read them, not reopen them, unless someone has something new.

What the room must settle. [Two / three / four] questions.

[Contested question 1, as a choice, e.g. "When (one good thing) competes with (another), which way do we lean?"]

[Contested question 2.]

[Contested question 3, if any.]

And the main effort itself. There are [number] candidates in the pre-read. Every one is built from your words. I used AI to help sort what you said, and I chose and rewrote the candidates myself. I haven't picked one. That call belongs to this team, and we'll make it together in the room.

[Direction: slower. This is the part people forget.]

You'll see where views differ. You won't see who held which view, and I'd ask you not to go looking. Each of you sees the company from a different seat, and each seat sees something true. In the room, speak for yourself.

How to come ready. Three things.

One: read the pre-read once, the day before. It's [two] pages. We won't read it together.

Two: for each open question, bring one real example from the last [six] months where it mattered. One real case settles more than an hour of argument.

Three: write one sentence and bring it. If [company] could only get one thing right this year, what is it, and what would you give up for it? Don't share it ahead. We'll use it early.

[Logistics, one line: cameras on, phones away, a short break at the halfway mark / directions and parking.]

The aim isn't a polished document. It's a page your people use on an ordinary Tuesday. See you on [Mirror date].

[Direction: cut. Send with the pre-read attached, not linked behind a login.]
