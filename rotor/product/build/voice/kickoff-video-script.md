# Kickoff video script

**Purpose:** one recording, sent in every company's welcome pack, so each leader knows what the program asks of them before their first interview.
**Target length:** about 3 minutes (roughly 430 words spoken).
**Per-client adapting:** none. No company, person or date is named. Re-record only if the program changes, or when it gets its real name at RP4.
**First recording:** about 15 minutes, one take.

**Before you record**
- [ ] Before recording, check that training is switched off in your AI account's settings (decided 2026-11-11).
- [ ] Plain background, everyday clothes.

---

[Direction: seated, eye level, talk to one person. Small stumbles are fine; leave them in. Burn in captions, since many will watch muted.]

Hi. I'm Brandon Franklin, from Franklin Kinetics. Your company has started the program, and this is a three-minute guide to what it asks of you.

The short version. Your company already has a strategy. The aim is to make it specific enough that your people, and the AI tools beside them, can act on it without asking. You know your business far better than I do. My job is to listen, reflect it back sharper, and help you make the hard calls.

[Direction: on screen, a plain timeline that builds as you speak: Build · Mirror · Live run · Review.]

It runs about five months.

First, roughly two months of build. Each of you talks with an AI interviewer, two or three times, about twenty minutes each. Whenever suits you, by voice or by typing. It asks how you see the company's priorities, its trade-offs, and what you're betting on. There are no right answers. When an answer is vague it asks a follow-up, and at the end it reads back what it heard so you can correct it.

Then a full quarter of live use with one team, the beta team. A second team, the comparison team, carries on as usual. That comparison is how we'll know whether anything really changed.

Then a review, and a decision: scale it, adjust it, or stop.

[Direction: hold up three fingers, or put "3 live moments" on screen.]

Only three moments are live. A kickoff with your CEO. The Mirror: two hours with the whole leadership team, where you see your strategy in your own words, settle the hard calls, and sign it off. And a review with your CEO at the end, after you've all watched the results. Everything else runs on your time. Your CEO gets a short written update [every week / every second week], and any question you send me gets an answer within three working days.

Now, what stays confidential. At the end of each conversation you send me a short summary, and I read every one. Nobody else at your company sees them, your CEO included. The full conversation stays in your own account; I don't receive it. What the leadership team sees is where views agree and where they differ, with nobody quoted by name unless you say so. I won't tell you what another leader said, and the interviewer won't either. Skip any question, stop any time, or ask me to leave something out. My working copies sit in a private project in my own AI account, with training switched off, and at the end I delete them and confirm that in writing. The AI vendor keeps deleted projects for up to [X] days.

[Direction: slower here. This is the part they'll act on.]

This week, three things.

One: put the Mirror date in your calendar and protect it. It's the one meeting that needs all of you.

Two: read the short note about the interviews. It says who sees what, in writing.

Three: think about where your strategy actually lives today. The plan, the board deck, the offsite notes, the thing people say in the lunchroom. If there's something you'd want me to read, send it to your CEO this week.

Your first interview invitation follows soon after.

Thanks for your time. I know it's the scarcest thing you've got. I'm looking forward to hearing how you see it.

[Direction: hold for two seconds, then cut.]
