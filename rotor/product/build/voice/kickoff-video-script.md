# Kickoff video script (Rotor Distillation)

**Purpose:** one recording, sent in every company's welcome pack, so each leader knows what the Rotor Distillation is, what it asks of them, and who sees what, before anything starts.
**Target length:** about 2.5 minutes (roughly 380 words spoken).
**Per-client adapting:** none. No company, person or date is named. Re-record only if the Distillation changes.
**First recording:** about 15 minutes, one take.

**Before you record**
- [ ] Check that training is switched off in your AI account's settings (decided 2026-11-11).
- [ ] Fill the vendor's retention period from its current policy: [X] days.
- [ ] Plain background, everyday clothes.

---

[Direction: seated, eye level, talk to one person. Small stumbles are fine; leave them in. Burn in captions, since many will watch muted.]

Hi. I'm Brandon Franklin, from Franklin Kinetics. Your company has started the Rotor Distillation, and this is a short guide to what it is and what it asks of you.

The short version. Your leadership team agrees on a strategy. Your AI tools have never seen it. Every day your people ask an AI for help, and it answers without knowing what you've decided. The Distillation fixes that. It starts from what you already have, your plan, your EOS or OKR pages, your offsite notes, and boils it down to one page an AI tool can act on. Distilling adds nothing. It takes out what isn't strategy and keeps what is.

[Direction: on screen, a plain timeline that builds as you speak: Distill · Load and test · Six weeks live · Review.]

It runs about twelve weeks.

The first four, we distill. I read what your documents say, your CEO talks with an AI interviewer, and I draft your Rotor Sheet: your main effort, the trade-offs you accept, and the beliefs your plan rests on. Around week three, each of you gets the draft. Read it once and send a short written reaction. The question is simple: would you act on this?

Then your CEO signs it off, your IT person loads it into your AI tools, and we test it against real dilemmas from one team before anyone relies on it.

Then six weeks of live use with that team, and a review.

[Direction: slower. This line matters.]

One thing to be clear on. The main effort is yours. I help you say it more sharply. I don't choose it, and neither does the AI. Where the documents leave a gap, I flag it. I don't fill it in.

Now, who sees what. Your strategy documents are read inside your own AI. I get a structured digest, not the documents. If you do an interview, you send me the short summary at the end, and I read it. The full conversation stays in your own account; I never receive it. Nobody is quoted by name unless they say so. My working copies sit in a private project in my own AI account, with training switched off, and at the end I delete them and confirm that in writing. The AI vendor keeps deleted content for up to [X] days.

[Direction: hold up two fingers.]

This week, two things.

One: read the short note about the interviews. It says who sees what, in writing.

Two: if there's a document, a page or a saying that captures how this company really decides, send it to your CEO this week, so it goes into the reading.

Any question you send me gets an answer within three working days.

Thanks for your time. I know it's the scarcest thing you've got.

[Direction: hold for two seconds, then cut.]
