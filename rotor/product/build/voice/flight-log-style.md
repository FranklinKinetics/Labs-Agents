# Update house style (internal name: the flight log)

The update is the CEO's short written note. It replaces a status meeting. It shows how the Rotor Sheet is behaving in real work, and it gives the CEO one useful thing to think about.

**Client-facing name:** "update", always. "Flight log" is the internal name, from the playbook; it never appears in anything the client sees.
**When:** every two weeks through the six weeks of live use (weeks 5 to 11, three updates). Same day every time: [day]. Weeks 1 to 4 have no update; the kickoff and sign-off calls cover them.
**To:** the CEO only.
**Length:** under 300 words. Aim for 200.
**Who writes it:** A9 drafts it. You read every line and put it in your own words. Budget: 8 minutes.
**Optional:** a 3-to-5-minute phone video, only when there's something to show.

## The four parts, always in this order

1. **What we heard.** From the decision log, the in-use ratings and, once it's run, the pulse. By team, in the team's own recurring words. Never who said what.
2. **What's taking shape.** How the guidance is behaving: where it helped, where it got in the way, where people overrode it. Said provisionally: "so far", "it looks like".
3. **What's next.** Dates and who does what: the pulse, the results pack, the review. If the CEO has to act, say what and by when.
4. **One question.** Something only the CEO can answer, in a sentence or two. A real question, not a quiz or a nudge in disguise.

## Rules

- **Nothing traceable to one person.** If only one person raised something, leave it out or say it so it can't be traced. On a team of five, even "someone in dispatch" can be a name.
- **Counts, not names, for progress.** "Four of six have answered the pulse." If someone is behind, you nudge the team lead yourself. The CEO is not the enforcer.
- **Team-level only.** Pulse findings are reported by team, never by person.
- **No early verdicts.** No "it's working", no percentages before the results pack, no money.
- **The main effort isn't up for grabs.** If the decision log shows people disagreeing with the main effort itself, say so plainly and save it for the review. Never rewrite it in an update.
- **Their words, not ours.** Use the company's language. Keep Rotor terms to the ones they've already met: main effort, guardrail, the Rotor Sheet.
- **Plain text.** Short sentences. No attachments needed to understand it.
- **Subject line:** `[company] · week [n] update`.
- **Sign off with your first name.** No "Best regards".

## Example (live use, first update)

> **Subject:** [company] · week [n] update
>
> [CEO first name],
>
> **What we heard.** The [beta team] has had the sheet in their AI tools for two weeks. [Number] moments are in the decision log so far. [Theme one, using a phrase the team keeps using, e.g. "(their recurring phrase)".] [Theme two, one sentence.]
>
> **What's taking shape.** It looks like the guidance is most useful on [type of decision]. On [type of decision], people are overriding it, mostly because [reason, if the log gives one]. Too early to say whether that's the sheet or the setup.
>
> **What's next.** The pulse goes to the team on [day]. Your next update is [date]. [Anything you need to do, with a date.]
>
> **One question.** [A question only you can answer, e.g. "When (situation from the log) came up, would you have made the same call the AI pointed to?"]
>
> [Brandon]
