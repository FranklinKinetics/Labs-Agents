# Rotor Product · RP4 offer (consulting side)

Fenn, 2026-11-11. Draft for Brandon's decision at Rotor Product Gate 4 (Package). Sources: the [RP2 spec](../rp2-spec.md) sections 1–4 and 8, the [dry-run plan](../build/checklists/dry-run-plan.md) stage 2, the client pack, and `logs/decisions.md` to 2026-11-11.

## 1. Name

The name has to sit in one breath with the other two rungs: *the alignment diagnostic, ____, the Rotor Beta.*

| Candidate | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **the Rotor Term** *(recommended)* | Says the one thing that makes it different, honestly: it starts on set dates, like a school term, so everyone can plan around them. It also matches how the calendar actually works (dates booked a term ahead). | "Term" is a busy word: contract terms, "Rotor terms" in our own glossary. Read cold, it can sound a little academic. |
| **the Rotor Quarter** | Names the heart of it: one team, one live quarter, measured against a comparison team. Concrete and businesslike. | The whole thing runs about five months, not three, so the name undersells the build. The Beta also has a live quarter, so it doesn't tell the two rungs apart. |
| **the Rotor Season** | Borrows from the grove imagery already on the site: a set start, a growing stretch, a harvest at the review. Warm, and memorable. | Soft. A CEO may hear "seasonal" or "once a year", and it says nothing about what they get. |

**Recommendation: the Rotor Term.** It explains fixed start dates as a feature of how the work is organized, not as a sales device, and it doesn't make the Beta sound like the "real" one. Used in a sentence: *"The next Rotor Term starts in January."*

*Avoided on purpose:* anything with "pilot", "flight", "mission", "squadron", "wing" or "cohort" (cohort implies a group that meets).

## 2. The ladder, for a buyer: "Which one is for me?"

*(119 words with the heading. For the website and the end of a discovery call.)*

> **Which one is for me?**
>
> It depends how much you want to know first, and how much of me you want in the room.
>
> **The alignment diagnostic** shows where your leaders agree and where they don't. You run it yourselves; it's useful even if you stop there.
>
> **The Rotor Term** is the full build: each leader heard on their own time, the hard calls settled at the Mirror, then one team working from it for a quarter. I read every interview and make every judgment. It starts on set dates.
>
> **The Rotor Beta** is the same journey with more of me in it: longer live sessions, faster replies, a start date we agree together. I take one a year.

## 3. Offer sheet: the Rotor Term

*What Brandon sends after a discovery call. One page. Brackets are for Brandon to fill.*

> # The Rotor Term
> **Franklin Kinetics · for [COMPANY] · after our conversation on [DATE]**
>
> ## What it is
> Your strategy is written down. It isn't yet steering the small decisions your people make every day, with or without an AI tool at their elbow. The Rotor Term turns it into one main effort, clear rules for the trade-offs you'll accept, and the beliefs your plan rests on, each with a warning sign. Then it puts that strategy where decisions happen: in your people's heads and in the AI tools they already use. One team works from it for a full quarter, and we measure what changed.
>
> ## Who it's for
> - Owner-led or founder-led companies of roughly 20 to 200 people.
> - A leadership team willing to say, separately and honestly, what it really thinks.
> - People who already use AI tools at work (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini).
> - It works alongside EOS, Scaling Up or OKRs. It doesn't replace them.
>
> ## What you get
> - **Your Rotor Sheet:** the main effort, trade-off rules with worked examples, and the beliefs behind your plan with their warning signs, in your company's own words. Signed off by your leadership team.
> - **A short strategy video** in your voice. I draft the script; you record it.
> - **Your beta team's AI tools set up** to work from your Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core, and tested against real dilemmas from that team before they go live.
> - **Regular progress notes** to you, so there's no status meeting.
> - **A results pack and a short video from me**, comparing your beta team with a comparison team, before we meet to decide what's next.
>
> ## How it runs
> About 21 weeks, roughly five months. Three moments are live. Everything else runs on your time.
>
> | When | Live moment | Who |
> |---|---|---|
> | Week 1 | **Kickoff**, 45 minutes, late afternoon (4 pm) | You and me |
> | About week 8 | **The Mirror**, 2 hours. Your strategy reflected back in your leaders' own words; the contested calls settled; the Rotor Sheet signed off. Remote by default. | Your leadership team and me |
> | About week 21 | **Review**, 60 minutes, late afternoon. Scale it, adjust it or stop. | You and me |
>
> Between those, your leaders each have two or three short conversations with an AI interviewer, about 20 minutes each, whenever suits them. I read every transcript. Every message you send me gets a reply within three working days, and anything you flag as urgent is answered first.
>
> **Why the start dates are fixed:** I do this work around other commitments, and I book the Mirror days well ahead so I can be fully present for them. The Mirror date is set the day you sign, so your leadership team can hold it too. If the next start date doesn't suit you, or it's already full, I'll say so plainly and offer the one after. While you wait, the alignment diagnostic gives your team a useful head start.
>
> ## What we'll ask of your team
> - **You:** the kickoff, the review, a few minutes a week with the progress notes, and recording the strategy video.
> - **Your leaders:** two or three short AI interviews each, a reaction to the draft, and the Mirror.
> - **A beta team of at least five people**, named at kickoff, who work from the strategy for the quarter and answer a few short questions each month. At least five, so no result ever points at one person.
> - **A similar comparison team** that carries on as normal. Both teams answer the same set of realistic decision cases before and after.
> - **Whoever looks after your IT:** one short checklist to set up the interviewer.
>
> ## Your data, in plain words
> - Your leaders' interviews run in your company's own AI platform, under your existing agreement with that vendor.
> - I do my drafting (summaries, progress notes, the Sheet) in my own AI account, in one private project used only for your company. Training on that account's content is switched off in its settings, and I confirm that in writing before the first interview.
> - At the end, I delete that project and every copy I hold, and send you written confirmation.
> - Quotes from your leaders are never attributed unless that leader agrees. Results are reported by team, never about a person, and are never used in performance reviews.
> - Anything you name at kickoff (acquisitions, people decisions, legal matters, unannounced numbers) stays out of every interview and every document.
>
> ## What it doesn't promise
> No financial results. Strategy takes longer than a quarter to show on the books, and I won't pretend otherwise. What we measure is how consistently your people decide, how well they know the strategy, and two or three measures we agree at kickoff. I report what happened, including where nothing changed.
>
> ## How to start
> Reply to this note, or **[Start a conversation](https://franklinkinetics.com/contact)**. If it's a fit, you get a written proposal with the scope and the next start date. Once you sign, your Mirror date is fixed and we begin.
>
> Brandon Franklin · Franklin Kinetics · Regina, Saskatchewan

## 4. Founding intake wording

*For the first two companies only. Brandon says this in the discovery call and repeats it in writing before signing. It never mentions another company: to each client, this is simply the first start date.*

> **You'd be on the first run.**
>
> I want to be straight with you before you decide. The Rotor Term is new. The method behind it draws on the Rotor Beta and on well-tested work on strategy and decision-making, but this format has not run with a client before. Yours would be the first start date.
>
> **What that means for you**
> - Everything on the offer sheet still applies: the three live moments, the reply standard, the data rules and the written deletion at the end.
> - Some of the materials will change as we go. When something doesn't work, I'll fix it and tell you what I changed.
> - I've kept extra room in my calendar for this first run, so you're not competing for my attention with a new Beta client.
> - I track my own time on every step. That's how I find out what the program really takes, and it's my problem, not yours.
>
> **What I'd ask in return**
> - **Honest feedback, as we go and at the end.** At the close, three short questions to your leaders: did you feel heard, was there enough of me, and what was annoying. Blunt answers help most.
> - **That's all.** Any testimonial, quote or write-up about your company would come only later, only if you want to, and only with your separate written permission. Saying no changes nothing about your program.

*Fenn's notes for Vera:* no discount, "special terms" or "founding rate" language anywhere, because each would imply a price. "First run" is used instead of "pilot". The calendar line is true under the dry-run plan (no Beta client in parallel with the founding build); it says nothing about other Term companies.
