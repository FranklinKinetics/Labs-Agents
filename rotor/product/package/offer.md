## 1. Name

The name has to sit in one breath with the other two rungs: *the alignment diagnostic, ____, the Rotor Beta.*

| Candidate | Why it works | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **the Rotor Term** *(recommended)* | Says the one thing that makes it different, honestly: it starts on set dates, like a school term, so everyone can plan around them. It also matches how the calendar actually works (dates booked a term ahead). | "Term" is a busy word: contract terms, "Rotor terms" in our own glossary. Read cold, it can sound a little academic. |
| **the Rotor Quarter** | Names the heart of it: one team, one live quarter, measured against a comparison team. Concrete and businesslike. | The whole thing runs about five months, not three, so the name undersells the build. The Beta also has a live quarter, so it doesn't tell the two rungs apart. |
| **the Rotor Season** | Borrows from the grove imagery already on the site: a set start, a growing stretch, a harvest at the review. Warm, and memorable. | Soft. A CEO may hear "seasonal" or "once a year", and it says nothing about what they get. |

> **Decided 2026-11-11 (RP4): the program is called the Rotor Distillation.** "Rotor Term" was rejected. The options below are kept for the record.

**Original recommendation: the Rotor Term.** It explains fixed start dates as a feature of how the work is organized, not as a sales device, and it doesn't make the Beta sound like the "real" one. Used in a sentence: *"The next Rotor Term starts in January."*

*Avoided on purpose:* aviation and military words, and "cohort" (it implies a group that meets).

## 2. The ladder, for a buyer: "Which one is for me?"

*(About 160 words with the heading. For the website and the end of a discovery call. The rungs are sorted by one question, whether the leaders already agree, and never by price or by how much of Brandon.)*

> **Which one is for me?**
>
> It comes down to one question: do your leaders already agree on where the company is going?
>
> **Not sure? The alignment diagnostic** finds out. Each leader answers on their own, and you see where you agree and where you don't. You run it yourselves, and it's useful even if you stop there.
>
> **If you agree: the Rotor Distillation.** You have a strategy, on EOS, OKRs or a plan of your own, and your leaders stand behind it. Your AI tools have never seen it. We distill what you've already decided into one page, load it into the tools your people use, test it, and put it to work with one team.
>
> **If you don't: the Rotor Beta.** When the split is the real problem, the AI can wait. The Beta settles the hard calls with your leadership team first, then carries the result to your people and your AI tools.
>
> If you'd rather talk it through, start with a conversation. I'll tell you plainly which one fits, including when it's neither.

## 3. Offer sheet: the Rotor Distillation

*What Brandon sends after a discovery call. One page. Brackets are for Brandon to fill.*

> # The Rotor Distillation
> **Franklin Kinetics · for [COMPANY] · after our conversation on [DATE]**
>
> ## What it is
> Your leaders agree on the strategy. Your AI tools have never seen it. Every day your people ask ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot for help with decisions, and the answers come from a tool that knows nothing about what you've decided.
>
> The Rotor Distillation starts from the strategy you already have: your plan, your EOS or OKR pages, your offsite notes, the things people say in the hallway. Distilling adds nothing. It takes out what isn't strategy and keeps what is. What's left is your **Rotor Sheet**: your main effort in plain words, the trade-offs you'll accept, and the beliefs your plan rests on, each with a warning sign. Then it goes where decisions happen, into the AI tools your people already use, and one team works with it for six weeks.
>
> **The main effort is yours.** I help you say it more sharply. I don't choose it, and neither does the AI. Where your documents leave a gap or contradict each other, I flag it for you; I don't fill it in. Your CEO signs off every line.
>
> ## Who it's for
> - Owner-led or founder-led companies of roughly 20 to 200 people.
> - A leadership team that broadly agrees on where the company is going, and has written it down somewhere.
> - People who already use AI tools at work.
> - It works alongside EOS, Scaling Up or OKRs. It doesn't replace them.
>
> ## First, a fit check
> Before you sign, we check that your leaders really do agree. If you've taken the alignment diagnostic, its report is the check. If not, your CEO and one or two leaders each have one 20-minute conversation with an AI interviewer, in your own AI, and I read the short summaries. If your leaders' main efforts clearly differ, I'll tell you so, and recommend the Rotor Beta or the diagnostic first. That's my call to make, and I make it myself.
>
> ## What you get
> - **Your Rotor Sheet**, distilled from your own strategy, in your company's own words, and signed off by your CEO.
> - **Your beta team's AI tools set up** to work from the Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core, and tested against ten real dilemmas from that team before anyone relies on them.
> - **A plain-language summary of how the AI behaves**, and a short note for your staff, so everyone can see what it works from.
> - **An update every two weeks** during the live weeks, so there's no status meeting.
> - **A results pack** before we meet to decide what's next.
>
> ## How it runs
> About 12 weeks. Three calls, all with your CEO, in the evening or late afternoon. Everything else runs on your time.
>
> | When | Call | Who |
> |---|---|---|
> | Week 1 | **Kickoff**, 45 minutes. Scope, what's off limits, which team, which AI tools, what we'll measure. | Your CEO and me |
> | About week 4 | **Sign-off**, 60 minutes. Open questions settled; your CEO signs off the main effort and the Rotor Sheet. | Your CEO and me |
> | About week 12 | **Review**, 60 minutes. Keep it, extend it to more teams, set up a quarterly review of the sheet, or stop. | Your CEO and me |
>
> Between the calls: your leaders read the draft sheet and send a short written reaction. Around week 5, your IT person loads the sheet into your AI tools, and the beta team lead writes ten real dilemmas to test it against. Then six weeks of live use. Every message you send me gets a reply within three working days, and anything you flag as urgent is answered first.
>
> **Starts are rolling.** There are no fixed start dates. We begin when you're ready. If I can't start when you'd like, I'll say so plainly and give you the earliest date I can.
>
> ## What we'll ask of your team
> - **Your CEO:** the three calls, one 20-minute AI interview, and running your strategy documents through a reading guide in your own AI, then emailing me the digest it produces.
> - **Your leaders:** a short written reaction to the draft. One or two may also do a 20-minute interview.
> - **A beta team of at least five people**, named at kickoff, who work with the sheet for six weeks and answer one short set of questions along the way. At least five, so no result ever points at one person. Its lead writes the ten test dilemmas.
> - **Whoever looks after your IT:** time to load the sheet into your AI tools, using a step-by-step guide, around week 5.
>
> ## Your data, in plain words
> - Your leaders' interviews, and the reading of your strategy documents, run in your company's own AI platform, under your existing agreement with that vendor.
> - I receive two things by email: the short summary at the end of each interview, and a structured digest of your strategy documents. Your AI produces both. If you took the alignment diagnostic, I also receive your report, if you choose to send it. I never receive your documents or the full conversations.
> - I do my drafting in my own AI account (a one-person plan), in one private project used only for your company. Training on that account's content is switched off in its settings, and I confirm that in writing before the first interview.
> - At close-out, I delete that project, the emailed summaries, the digest and every copy I hold, and send you written confirmation. The AI vendor may keep deleted content for [X days, from the vendor's current policy].
> - The Rotor Sheet and the Rotor Core go into your AI tools under licence.
> - Quotes from your leaders are never attributed unless that leader agrees. Results are reported by team, never about a person, and are never used in performance reviews.
> - Anything you name at kickoff (acquisitions, people decisions, legal matters, unannounced numbers) stays out of every interview and every document.
>
> ## What it doesn't promise
> No financial results. Six weeks can't show up on the books, and I won't pretend otherwise. What we look at is how good the AI's guidance is in your team's own judgment, whether your people can name the main effort and two of its guardrails, the moments where the guidance changed a call, and where people overrode it. I report what happened, including where nothing changed.
>
> ## How to start
> Reply to this note, or **[Start a conversation](https://franklinkinetics.com/contact)**. If the fit check says go, you get a written proposal with the scope and a start date.
>
> Brandon Franklin · Franklin Kinetics · Regina, Saskatchewan

## 4. Founding-client wording

*For the first companies only. Brandon says this in the discovery call and repeats it in writing before signing. It never names or mentions another company.*

> **You'd be among the first.**
>
> I want to be straight with you before you decide. The Rotor Distillation is new. The method behind it draws on the Rotor Beta and the Rotor Framework, but this format hasn't run with a client before. Yours would be among the first companies to take it.
>
> **What that means for you**
> - Everything on the offer sheet still applies: the fit check, the three calls, the reply standard, the data rules and the written deletion at the end.
> - Some of the materials will change as we go. When something doesn't work, I'll fix it and tell you what I changed.
> - I track my own time on every step. That's how I find out what the work really takes, and it's my problem, not yours.
>
> **What I'd ask in return**
> - **Honest feedback, as we go and at the end.** At the close, three short questions to your CEO and the beta team lead: was the sheet true to your strategy, did the AI's guidance help, and what was annoying. Blunt answers help most.
> - **The rest is your choice.** Any testimonial, quote or write-up about your company would come only later, if you want it, and only with your separate written permission. Saying no changes nothing about your Distillation.

*Fenn's notes for Vera:* no discount, credit, "special terms" or "founding rate" language anywhere, because each would imply a price. The calendar line no longer compares with Beta clients, so it says nothing about how many there are. The three feedback questions changed from the old ones ("did you feel heard", "was there enough of me"), which were written for the leader-by-leader build.
